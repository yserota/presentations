Slide 2 Our Strategy Target: 90 sec

“Before I show you the results, I want to frame the strategy — because everything I’m about to show you is a direct consequence of it.

The direction is this: documentation stops being something a writer produces, and becomes something the organization governs. AI does the production. Writers do the judgment.

We are in Stage 1 now. 12 AI skills in production — skills that draft, review, score, and route content automatically. The team uses them every day, on every deliverable. The efficiency gains that followed are real, and I’ll show you the numbers on the next slide.

Stage 2 is Q2 — two tracks. First: TW skills embedded into AION, R&D’s development pipeline. Documentation gets triggered automatically when code ships — no writer needs to be in the loop to start the process. Second: the Doc Health initiative. Every documentation page gets an AI-generated health score. We piloted 24 high-traffic pages, and we’re scaling to 200+ in Q2. The score becomes a publishing gate — quality is measured, not assumed.

Stage 3 is the future state: engineering ships, AI drafts, scores, and publishes. Writers architect the system and judge the output — a small senior team governing a fully automated content pipeline.

The metrics on the next slide are the proof that Stage 1 is working.”

Why this works
Slide 2 is the strategy frame, not the proof. Keep it at the level of what you’re doing and why — Peretz needs the model before he can evaluate the numbers. The metrics land harder on Slide 3 because the context is already set. Don’t preview specific numbers here; let “the proof is on the next slide” do the work.
Likely Q & A

Likely Q & A

Slide 3 How We Measure Success Target: 60 sec

“Before I show you what we built, here’s how we know it’s working.

Four metrics — and I want to give you the actual numbers, not targets.

AI-assisted output: twelve months ago, zero percent of our output was AI-assisted. Today we’re at 97% — measured against every resolved ticket in Jira. That’s not a pilot number. That’s the full team, every deliverable, every quarter.

Cycle time: we cut it from 19 days to 6 days — a 68% reduction. The target is 3 days by Q2.

Quality Score: 24 high-traffic pages scored and piloted. Every single one has a writer-opened PR in progress. We’re scaling to 200+ pages in Q2, and the health score becomes a publishing gate.

And the fourth — the one that proves everything else: in early November we’re reducing the team from 19 to 14 FTEs while maintaining delivery output. AI is absorbing the capacity. That’s not a projection — it’s what the data makes possible.

These four numbers are our contract with ourselves. Everything I’m about to show you connects back to moving one of these.”

Why this works
The 0%→97% arc in 12 months is the most compelling single data point in the deck — let it land before moving on. The 97% and 68% figures are sourced from Jira and DCI data, so they’re defensible. Peretz evaluates every slide that follows against a framework you defined here.

“Where does the 97% number come from?”

Slide 4 Key Achievements of Q1 FY2027 Target: 90 sec

“Let me walk you through what we actually shipped this quarter.

The headline: 12 AI skills in production. That includes a Release Notes Agent, an AI-powered Product Documentation Writer and Reviewer, and a Bulk Jira Ticket Creator. These are not prototypes — they’re running in the team’s daily workflow today.

Second: we launched the What’s New feed — a live, auto-updating stream that unifies all the latest product release announcements. That’s a content category that was previously a maintenance burden.

Third: telemetry — two examples. User behavior: MixPanel is now mapped to the What’s New feed, giving us real data on what content users actually engage with. Quality telemetry: we have a scoring and ranking system for documentation health. 24 highest-traffic pages are in the pilot. Every page has a health score, a priority rank, and a writer assigned.

And MkDocs migration is underway — the infrastructure shift that will make our content machine-readable and AI-ingestible at scale. That’s a Q2 completion target.

Three major deliverables shipped. One strategic program in motion.”

Why this works
Lead with the 12 skills — that’s the concrete proof of AI output. The specific skill names (Release Notes Agent, Doc Writer/Reviewer, Bulk Jira) make it tangible to a CPO, not abstract.
Likely Q & A
“How do you know the skills actually save time?”
“97% of our resolved tickets this quarter had AI-assisted output — that’s up from 85% in Q4. Cycle time dropped from 19 days to 6. The throughput is the proof.”
Watch out for
Don’t over-explain MkDocs. If asked, it’s the doc infrastructure shift that enables AI ingestion — the prerequisite for Pillar 2 in Q2.
Slide 5 Demo: AI Skills in Action Target: 3–4 min (live demo)

“I want to show you these skills, not just describe them. I’m going to demo three that are live in our workflow right now.

First: tw-doc-write — the AI documentation writer. You give it a feature spec or Jira ticket, it drafts the doc.

Second: tw-doc-review — the AI reviewer. It reads a draft against our style guide and flags issues, just like a senior writer would in a code review.

Third: open-pr — the PR skill. Once the doc is ready, it opens the pull request automatically, with the right metadata, branch naming, and reviewers.

What you’re seeing is an end-to-end workflow: spec in, published doc out, human judgment at the review gate.”

Why this works
A live demo in an exec QBR is a bold move — it signals confidence. Frame it as “this is running today” before handing to Ben. Don’t over-set-up; let the demo speak.
If demo goes sideways
“The output I showed earlier was generated by exactly this pipeline. Here’s a saved run.” Have a screen recording ready as backup.
Watch out for
Keep the narration tight. Peretz cares about what it replaces and how fast — not the technical steps. Keep returning to: “A writer used to spend X hours on this.”
Slide 6 Getting to the North Star — Together Target: 75 sec

“Reaching our goal requires partners — not just our team executing. Here’s where the four cross-org programs stand.

With Customer Support and Knowledge Management: these are both part of the Supportability Documentation Program — a high-visibility initiative being tracked at the executive level by Nikesh and Sonny. On the Support side, AI surfaces gaps from high-impact escalation cases and writers close them. On the KB side, we’re running a POC that identifies where customers are forced to learn from stale KB articles because the official docs don’t answer the question — and fixing that at the source.

With Product: AI ecosystem for plugin content on the Marketplace. In progress. Partners and third-party contributors will get the same AI-assisted content layer our own writers use.

And we’re now collaborating with the PANDA team to ingest Idira documentation into PANDA’s knowledge base. PANDA is the company’s agentic AI assistant — when employees ask it IT, HR, or Legal questions, we want the answers to come from authoritative, maintained docs, not stale KB articles. In progress.

The pattern across all four: AI makes organizational scale possible without adding headcount.”

Why this works
Name-dropping Nikesh and Sonny as executive sponsors of the Supportability program tells Peretz this is already on leadership’s radar — he doesn’t need to champion it, he just needs to see TW’s role in it. Then end with PANDA as the company-infrastructure closer.

Slide 7 Q2 FY2027 — Roadmap Target: 90 sec

“Q2 is three pillars — each one building on what we delivered in Q1.

Pillar 1: Docs in the Agentic Lifecycle. TW Skills integrated into AION, R&D’s ADLC platform. And beyond the skills, we’re adding a formal TW persona into the cycle itself — so the system knows when to invoke a writer’s judgment, which standards apply, and how to route the output. We go from reactive to embedded.

Pillar 2: Product Knowledge Infrastructure. Three deliverables: MkDocs plus Cora AI ingestion, Quality at Scale across 200+ pages, and MixPanel analytics embedded across the full docs site. Every page will have engagement data. Prioritization stops being instinct — it’s driven by user behavior.

Pillar 3: Efficiency at Scale. This is the one that directly answers the question leadership is asking. As AI absorbs production, the Standards and Operations Hub — the SOH — becomes the engine. A lean senior team that sets standards, governs quality, and architects the pipeline. Continued headcount reduction. Same output. Better output.

Three pillars. One direction. Q2 is execution.”

Why this works
Pillar 3 is the one Peretz is waiting for — the efficiency and headcount story. Saving it for last gives it the most weight. “Same output. Better output.” is the key line. Pause after it.
