Slide 2 North Star Target: 90 sec

“Before I show you what we built, I want to show you where we’re going — because it puts everything else in context.

The north star is this: documentation stops being something a writer produces, and becomes something the organization governs. AI does the production. Writers do the judgment.

We are currently in Stage 1 — AI assists the writer. 12 skills shipped, telemetry live, cross-org programs running. That’s Q1.

Stage 2 is Q2 — TW skills get integrated into AION, the R&D ADLC, and quality gets a benchmark — a defined standard every page is measured against.

Stage 3 is where this is going: engineering ships a feature, AI generates the first draft from the spec, the health pipeline scores it before it goes live, and Cora AI ingests it automatically. Writers architect the system — they don’t produce the content.

And here’s why this matters to you specifically: Cora AI is only as trustworthy as what it ingests. If the knowledge base is inconsistent and unscored — Cora AI is unreliable by design. This program and the Cora AI product strategy are the same bet.”

Why this works
Opens with destination before journey. The Cora AI hook makes everything that follows feel strategic, not operational.
Likely Q & A
“When does Stage 3 happen?”
“Stage 3 requires Stage 2, and Stage 2 requires the RAG infrastructure decision. That’s one of the things I want your input on today.”
Slide 3 How We Measure Success Target: 60 sec

“Before I show you what we built, here’s how we know it’s working.

Four metrics. AI-assisted output at 75–80% — that’s the share of content touched by AI before a writer reviews it. Cycle time at three days or less, down from twelve. Quality Score — every high-traffic page gets a green score by Q2. And the fourth: we are reducing the team by five positions while maintaining delivery output — AI is absorbing the capacity gap.

These four numbers are our contract with ourselves. Everything I’m about to show you connects back to moving one of these.”

Why this works
Setting the metrics before the evidence means Peretz evaluates every slide against a framework you defined — not one he invents. It signals rigor and ownership.
Likely Q & A
“How do you maintain delivery with fewer people?”
“AI skills are absorbing the recurring work. 12 skills in production means 12 workflows running without a writer. The team shifts from producing to governing.”

Slide 5 Getting to the North Star — Together Target: 75 sec

“One thing that changed this quarter is that TW stopped being a downstream team. We’re now embedded in four cross-organizational programs.

With Customer Support: we built a feedback loop so that high-priority escalations trigger a documentation work item — customer pain becomes a prioritized fix, not an anecdote.

With Knowledge Management: AI analyzes the support KB and surfaces what customers are learning from support articles instead of the official docs. That gap closes into the docs layer, where it scales to every customer.

With Legal: third-party license notice publishing — previously manual and error-prone — now runs automatically on every release cycle.

With Product: we’re building the AI publishing ecosystem for the Marketplace, so documentation quality extends to partners and external contributors, not just our team.

The pattern is the same in all four: AI makes organizational scale possible without removing human judgment.”

Why this works
Opens with the strategic shift — TW as platform — before listing programs. Each program gets one sentence. The closing pattern line makes this feel repeatable, not ad-hoc.
Likely Q & A
“How much of your team’s time do these programs take?”
“AI does the heavy lifting. Our role is to define standards and review output. Third-Party Notices went from writer-hours per release to near-zero once built.”
Slide 6 Spotlight: Doc Health Initiative Target: 75 sec

“Let me go one level deeper on Pillar 1, because this is the initiative I’m most excited about strategically.

The problem we solved: documentation quality has always been invisible. You couldn’t answer ‘is this page good?’ without a writer reading it. We changed that.

The pipeline is four steps: AI reads the page and scores it on a structured rubric. The score is combined with traffic data to produce a priority rank — so we fix the pages that hurt the most users first. The output goes into a machine-readable catalog. A writer reviews the AI-proposed fix and opens a PR.

The pilot: 24 highest-traffic pages. The top page — CyberArk REST Web Services API — gets 2,427 unique views. It scored ORANGE. There’s a PR open. Every single pilot page has a writer on it.

Ben Skelker is scaling this in Q2: close all 11 RED-tier pages, expand scoring to 200+ pages, and eventually make a health score a publishing gate — content doesn’t go live if it scores below threshold.

This is how you govern a knowledge base at scale.”

Why this works
Naming the top page with its UV count makes the abstract concrete. “Publishing gate” shows you’re thinking like a product manager.
Likely Q & A
“What does ORANGE mean? How bad is it?”
“ORANGE = significant issues but not broken. RED = a user following the page could fail. We have 11 RED pages, all with PRs open. Those are Q2 priority.”
